# Find your edge (about 10–15 minutes; short version 5)

Investors will ask "why you?". Your edge is the answer, and it decides which ideas are worth your next ten years. This step uses Entrepreneurs First's Edge framework, in EF's own terms, then tests it against evidence from your past.

## EF's framework, in brief

- **Edge:** "the unique combination of skills, knowledge, behaviors, and experiences that put you in a better position than almost anyone else to build a particular company." EF's three types:
  - **Tech Edge:** "expertise in a technical domain that others don't yet fully understand", and you see new ways to apply it.
  - **Market Edge:** "sufficient exposure to a particular industry to understand the status quo and want to challenge it". Too much time and "you become entrenched in the status quo".
  - **Catalyst Edge:** "the ability to bring people together, assemble resources, and generate momentum that others can't".
  - Tech and Market Edges help generate an idea; a Catalyst Edge helps you execute it. Rank your edges. ([EF, Dec 2025](https://www.joinef.com/posts/play-your-strongest-card/))
- **Edge comes first, then the idea:** "use that as a starting point to find a problem" ([Alice Bentinck, 2018](https://medium.com/entrepreneurs-first/ideas-pt-i-where-do-ideas-come-from-58ae761800a)). Once you are on an idea, you deepen the edge through the customer ([Bentinck, 2022](https://greylock.com/reid-hoffman/how-to-be-a-founder/)).
- **The tests EF uses:**
  - **Outsider test:** "could someone outside of the industry have guessed this? If yes, you need to go back to the drawing board."
  - **Founder comparison:** compare yourself with "the rest of the founder population", not your peers.
  - **Edge is not passion:** "Make sure you don't confuse Edge with passion." ([Bentinck, 2018](https://medium.com/entrepreneurs-first/ideas-pt-ii-finding-your-edge-8808121b591b))
- **For repeat founders,** XF's own line is "Scar tissue is an unfair advantage", and it backs "a track record of correct, contrarian calls" ([joinxf.com](https://www.joinxf.com/)).

Strengths, unfair access, anti-edges and the idea fit score below are this folder's additions, not EF terms. Say so if the founder asks.

## Rules
- **Mine before you ask.** Never ask a question the files already answer.
- **Ask one question at a time** and wait. Ask for a specific story: when, who, what happened, what the number was.
- **Evidence beats opinion.** From weakest to strongest: "I'm good at X", then a role or title, then an artefact or a number, then someone else's behaviour (they paid, hired you, followed you, came back, keep asking you).
- **Collect, don't write.** In setup, `context/edge.md` joins the single proposal at the end. Run on its own ("find my edge"), propose it and write it after approval.

## 1. Mine the past (no questions yet, about 2 minutes)

Read everything available:
- `context/linkedin.md` (or the PDF). Recommendations count: they show what people come to the founder for.
- Answers so far, and any existing context files, notes, goals or old strategy docs (Mode B).
- Past company material: decks, investor updates, post-mortems, `ideas/killed/`.
- Anything they have written (posts, essays, talks), if they point you to it.

Then show a draft in about 10 lines:
- 2–4 candidate edges, each with its EF type, the evidence (file and detail) and a rating of strong, medium or weak;
- likely strengths and unfair access;
- one or two possible anti-edges.

End with: "This is my guess from your files. Let's test it."

## 2. Test it (one question at a time, 5–7 questions)

Ask the questions that would most change the draft. Start with the weakest or most important claim. Pick from:
1. "What did you see before others did? When, and what happened next?" (Tech or Market Edge; for repeat founders, a contrarian call that proved right)
2. "Where have you been unusually good, by evidence, not opinion? What's the number, or who noticed?"
3. "What access do you have that others don't: people who take your call, data, a channel, a buyer who trusts you?"
4. "What do people come to you for? What did they ask you for last month?"
5. "What did your last company teach you that is now rare knowledge? What would a smart outsider get wrong in that market?"
6. "What would you never do again, and what did it cost you last time?" (scar tissue becomes an edge only when it changes a decision)
7. "Where are you weaker than you would like a cofounder or investor to know?" (anti-edges)

## 3. Challenge weak edges

Push back once, firmly, then record what survives:
- **Generic traits** ("good leader", "hard-working", "fast learner", "great at sales"): "Most founders say that. What is the evidence that you're better than other founders at it?" With real proof it may be a Catalyst Edge; without proof it is a strength at best.
- **Interest or passion** ("I love fitness", "I care about climate"): "That's interest, not edge." Matt Clifford: "So are literally a billion other people" ([2022](https://greylock.com/reid-hoffman/how-to-be-a-founder/)). Ask what they know or can build there that few others can.
- **"I experienced this problem once":** EF calls this the quadrant where most first-time founders pick problems ([2016](https://medium.com/entrepreneurs-first/understanding-founder-idea-fit-f16d658c0e8f)). It is not an edge.
- **Years served, no insight** ("ten years in insurance"): run the outsider test. No surprising insight means no Market Edge yet, and long tenure may be an anti-edge (entrenched).
- **The old playbook:** "Is that true of this market, or was it true of your last one?"
- **Peer comparison** ("everyone in my team could do this"): compare with founders, not colleagues.

## 4. Output: `context/edge.md`

```markdown
# My edge
**Last updated:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Why me, in one line
The answer to "why you?" for the space I am in, with the proof.

## Edges, ranked (EF's types)
| Rank | Type (Tech / Market / Catalyst) | The edge, specifically | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Strengths, with proof
- Strength: the proof (a number, an artefact, or someone else's behaviour).

## Unfair access
Networks, data, distribution, buyers who take my call. Name them.

## Anti-edges and blind spots
Where I am a tourist, where I may be entrenched, and old-company habits that could mislead me. For each: what I do about it (cofounder, advisor, a test).

## Idea fit test
Score each idea in `ideas/` from 0 to 2 on each line (10 maximum), and write the result in its "Edge fit" section:
1. **Uses my top edge:** it rests on edge 1 or 2 above.
2. **Why me:** I can answer "why you?" in one sentence with evidence.
3. **Outsider test:** it rests on an insight an outsider could not have guessed.
4. **Access:** I can reach 10 real buyers through my own network this week.
5. **Why now:** my edge tells me why this is ripe now.
Subtract 2 if it leans on a listed anti-edge.
8–10: plays my strongest card. 5–7: a gap a cofounder or a test must close. Under 5: a tourist idea, so pursue it only as a deliberate choice, logged in `decisions.md`.
```

In `context/founder.md`, replace the old unfair-advantage line with one line pointing here.

## Short version (about 5 minutes)

Do step 1 in full. Ask only questions 1, 3 and 5, and challenge once. Write the same `context/edge.md`, and mark the thin sections "to test". Offer the full version later ("find my edge").
